The build

One robot.
Many hands.

The team starts with the competition rules and works backward. What must the robot do? What can we realistically build? What can we test before it matters?

Mechanical, electrical, and software work in parallel, then collide in the best way: the first full-system test. From there, every practice run exposes something new to fix.

Inside the robot

Four systems,
one machine.

Match day

All day
on your feet.

A competition is a build season in miniature: setup, safety checks, quick repairs, and a match clock that doesn't care how tired you are.

Students run the pit, drive the robot, talk to other teams, and make the calls that keep the bot in the bracket. It is the most stressful and most rewarding part of the year.

Pit crew
Repairs, battery swaps, and a parts kit that is actually organized
Drive team
Practice, communication, and a plan for every match
Safety
Weapons checked, batteries handled correctly, and a clean pit
The robot is never finished. It is only ready for the next match.

Safety first

Everybody home
in one piece.

Combat robotics involves real tools, real batteries, and real deadlines. Safety is the one part of the build that is never optional.

Before you touch a tool

Training first, safety glasses always, and a mentor sign-off before students use shop equipment.

Respect the power

Battery safety, proper fusing, and never working alone on a live system.

Check, then check again

Pre-match checklists, a clean pit, and the discipline to stop when something is not right.
